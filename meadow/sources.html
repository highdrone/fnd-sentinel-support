A thoughtful starting point

Sources & approach

Steady Meadow offers prompts for a personal journal and optional ideas for support. Its templates draw on the public resources below. They are starting points you choose, not diagnoses the app assigns.

Editorial sources checked September 6, 2026 · Version 3.0

What a template means

A template changes the wording, suggested symptom selections, and optional support ideas shown in the app. FND, anxiety, panic, and sensory overwhelm can involve different needs. Choosing a template does not establish what caused a symptom, and a symptom can have causes that the app cannot assess.

The journal records what you enter. Patterns summarizes those entries and available measurements. It does not infer a trigger, diagnose a condition, measure treatment success, or predict an episode. A day without a record remains unknown. A 14-day visit report brings your observations into a conversation; it is not a clinical assessment.

FND

The FND template emphasizes personal context, a care-team plan, questions for appointments, and explaining support preferences to trusted people. The source describes functional symptoms and routes to professional support.

Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust: Functional Neurological Symptoms

Anxiety & panic support

The anxiety and panic templates offer space to describe a moment, ask for support, and choose a manageable next step. They do not tell someone that unfamiliar physical symptoms are caused by panic.

NHS: Anxiety, fear and panic

Comfortable breathing

The optional breathing guide invites an easy rhythm without forcing a breath. It has no breath-hold requirement or score. A visual guide is a pacing option, not a measurement of breathing or a guarantee of relief. Skip it or stop if focusing on breathing is unhelpful.

NHS: Breathing exercises for stress

Noticing your surroundings

Grounding prompts offer a way to notice a few details around you. Choose senses that feel comfortable; there is no correct answer or requirement to finish. Manchester's CAMHS resource includes grounding and trusted-person support among its self-help suggestions.

Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust, CAMHS: Anxiety

Sensory overwhelm

The sensory template centers individual preferences: quieter surroundings when available, a familiar object or activity, and choice about company, touch, and instructions. The NHS England resource discusses adapting environments to sensory needs; it does not validate Steady Meadow or imply that everyone has the same needs.

NHS England: Sensory-friendly resource pack

Everyday wellbeing

The general template makes room for connection, an interest, and noticing an ordinary detail. Its prompts can be adapted to your energy and circumstances without choosing a diagnosis.

NHS: 5 steps to mental wellbeing

What these sources do not establish

The linked organizations have not endorsed this app. These are educational resources, not clinical trials of Steady Meadow. The app has not been clinically validated, and its journal, illustrations, tools, and Watch readings must not be treated as proof that someone is safe or improving.

Steady Meadow 3.0 does not expose the experimental prediction interface from earlier FND Sentinel builds or send automatic health alerts. Historical records may remain during an upgrade; they are not a prediction service. The earlier FND Sentinel methodology describes that predecessor, not the current support templates.

Choose only ideas that feel useful and fit your care plan. New, severe, or unfamiliar symptoms need medical advice; contact local emergency services when someone may be in danger. External resources open outside the app and follow the publisher's own privacy practices.
